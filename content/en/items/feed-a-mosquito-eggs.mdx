## Why Feed A Mosquito Eggs Drive Your Entire Early Game

Every cash-per-second empire in this game starts with a single larva lying on the grass. If you're hunting for Feed A Mosquito eggs and you're not sure which drop to grab or how long to stand there waiting for a lid to pop, this walkthrough covers the exact route recorded players used in their first session. Getting Feed A Mosquito eggs right — the pickup, the hatch, and the 100% feed that follows — is the difference between a mosquito that trickles a few coins and one that pays you every second while you're off fighting bosses.

The premise is simple: you are the food supply. The mosquitoes you own drink your blood, and well-fed mosquitoes pay you cash continuously. Eggs are the only way to add new earners to your base, which makes them the single most important item on your plot.

The best part for new players is that the entire opening loop is playable from the moment you spawn. Nothing in it waits on an unlock, a level gate, or a Robux purchase. Community reports confirm first sessions that completed kill → hatch → feed → collect → first weapon upgrade using nothing but the starting hand weapon and an empty base plot.

| Resource | Where it comes from | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Health (blood) | Sitting at your base seat | Feeding base mosquitoes and surviving field fights |
| Larvae / eggs | Drops from field mosquitoes | Hatching new mosquitoes for your base |
| Cash | Fed mosquitoes, collected at the green circle | Weapons and base upgrades |

One thing to internalize early: once a mosquito is placed at your base, it stays there. In player experience, a placed mosquito could not be brought into a field fight — it simply remains at the base earning. Don't build your combat plans around your best earner.

## The First-Session Route, Step by Step

Feed A Mosquito runs on Roblox, so make sure you have the [official Roblox player](https://www.roblox.com) installed before anything else. Once you're in, the opening is short and repeatable.

| # | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sit at your base seat | Refills health, which doubles as the blood you feed mosquitoes with |
| 2 | Attack a mosquito in the first field area | These standing mosquitoes act as starter bosses and drop larvae |
| 3 | Pick up a fallen larva | In the first area every drop is common, so any one works |
| 4 | Place the egg on your base plot | Any spot on your plot is valid; the egg hatches on a timer |
| 5 | Open the lid and place your mosquito | This is your first earner |
| 6 | Feed it with your blood | Drains health while feeding; health regenerates automatically |
| 7 | Walk over the green circle | Collects the cash your mosquitoes have earned |
| 8 | Buy the shoe | First weapon upgrade; makes the same kill dramatically faster |

A few details worth knowing before you start swinging:

- **Your seat is upgradeable.** It begins at level 1 with an energy-gain upgrade available, so early cash spent there improves both your feeding capacity and your field survivability.
- **Other players' kills still count.** Larvae dropped from someone else's fight can be picked up by anyone. One recorded player grabbed a purple, higher-rarity larva from another player's kill and counted it as help, not theft.
- **The loop is not Robux-gated.** The early weapon shop does list one Robux-exclusive item — mosquito spray — but it can be skipped entirely without slowing the route.

## What Different Feed A Mosquito Eggs Hatch Into

Not all Feed A Mosquito eggs behave the same way. Stronger field mosquitoes drop bigger larvae, and bigger larvae hatch into better earners — but they also take longer to open, which means more standing around while your income sits at zero.

| Drop type | Approx. hatch time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Common larva (first field area) | About 7 seconds | Every drop in the first area is common, so any larva works |
| Larger larva | Roughly 12 seconds in one recorded session | Bigger larvae tend to hatch into stronger earners |
| Larva from another player's kill | Varies by rarity | Anyone can pick it up; one player grabbed a purple drop this way |

Hatch times above are player experience from recorded sessions, not a fixed published table — treat them as a rough expectation rather than a guarantee.

The bigger variable is pay rate. A mosquito's income scales with its feeding percentage, and a freshly hatched mosquito pays only a fraction of its ceiling. The examples below come from community reports and single sessions, so use them as a feel for the range rather than a spreadsheet.

| Mosquito example | Pay rate | Context |
|---|---|---|
| First common mosquito | 18 cash per second | Measured at 100% fed |
| Other early mosquitoes | 24, 26, 30, and 50 per second | Observed across recorded runs once fully fed |
| Freshly hatched uncommon | 6 cash per second | Right after hatching, before feeding |
| A mosquito with a 100 cash ceiling | Starts at 10 per second | Shows how much income sits behind the feed bar |

The takeaway: the same egg can look disappointing at 5% and excellent at 100%. Feeding is not optional polish — it's the multiplier on every egg you hatch.

## Feeding Rhythm, Cash Banking, and Your First Upgrade

Feeding is a physical act in this game, not a menu click. A mosquito clamps onto you and drains your health while your health regenerates on its own. That creates a simple rhythm: sit to refill, feed until you're low, sit again. The mosquito visibly grows as its percentage climbs.

Three habits separate smooth runs from stalled ones:

1. **Feed every new hatch to 100% before you get attached to its number.** A half-fed mosquito still occupies a slot and still pays a fraction.
2. **Bank at the green circle constantly.** Money your mosquitoes earn waits at your base until you physically walk over the circle — it never accrues on its own. One recorded pickup collected almost 7,000 cash in a single walk-over.
3. **Ignore the selling station early.** It exists at the base, but nothing in your first session is worth selling.

Your HUD keeps things readable: the money counter sits in the left corner, while your equipped mosquitoes and hatchable eggs sit on the right.

Once cash is banked, reinvestment order matters more than the size of your wallet. Here's the weapon ladder as observed in recorded play:

| Weapon tier | Rough cost | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Hand (default) | Free | Slow kills on first-area mosquitoes |
| Shoe | First upgrade | The same first-area mosquito dies "much easier" |
| Baseball bat, frying pan, pot, sword, axe, hoe, katana, scythe | Varies, listed in the shop | Each tier speeds up field kills |
| A 5,000-cash weapon | 5,000 | Owned later in the same recorded session |
| The Turner | Above that tier | Within reach for a player who kept farming |
| Top shop tier | 35,000 | Left for later in the recorded run |

Health is the other half of the upgrade picture. One recorded player was comfortably re-fighting first-area mosquitoes at 600 health. Another, stuck at 150 health, couldn't beat his next target, estimated the housewife mosquito would require roughly 500 health, and tied that threshold to eventually earning 15,000 cash. If a fight feels impossible, the answer is usually more health, not better aim.

## Mistakes That Slow Down Your Feed A Mosquito Eggs Run

Most early stumbles come from the same handful of habits. Here's what to avoid and what to do instead.

| Mistake | Why it costs you | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving a new mosquito underfed | It pays a fraction of its maximum indefinitely | Sit, refill, and feed to 100% before hatching the next egg |
| Forgetting the green circle | Earned cash sits uncollected at your base | Walk over it every time you pass, and again before shopping |
| Buying mosquito spray early | Robux spent on something the free route skips | Save it; the hand and shoe cover the opening loop |
| Fighting above your health pool | You lose the fight and waste time | Farm first-area mosquitoes until your health supports the next tier |
| Ignoring the seat's energy gain | Slower refills mean slower feeding cycles | Funnel early cash into the seat upgrade |
| Treating your best earner as a fighter | Placed mosquitoes stay at base | Keep your top earner home and fight with your weapon |

On the Robux question: it's genuinely optional on this path. One player reached the full milestone — hatched, 100%-fed, paid mosquito plus a first weapon — spending zero Robux. Another bought blood multipliers (3x and up) purely to feed thirstier mosquitoes more comfortably, but still hatched and fed through the normal egg route.

A reasonable next goal after your first 100% mosquito and the shoe: keep farming first-area larvae, feed each new hatch to full, bank at the green circle, and push the seat's energy gain. A slightly stronger first-area mosquito was described as "right on the edge" of one player's health pool — a natural next fight — followed by the mosquito-mom tier, with the housewife mosquito waiting until you have several hundred health. Newer content such as Update V2's weapons, boss, and open PVP zone sits beyond a first session, so don't rush it.

## FAQ

**Where do you get Feed A Mosquito eggs?**
You get them from field mosquitoes. Attack one in the first field area until its health bar empties, then pick up the larva it drops. Every drop in the first area is common, and larvae left by other players' kills can be picked up by anyone.

**How long do Feed A Mosquito eggs take to hatch?**
Community reports put a common egg at roughly 7 seconds, with larger larvae taking longer — one larger egg was ready in about 12 seconds in a recorded session. Place the egg on your plot, wait for the timer, then open the lid to collect your mosquito.

**Do you need Robux to get Feed A Mosquito eggs?**
No. The full opening loop — kill, hatch, feed, collect, first weapon — was completed in recorded sessions with zero Robux spent. The one Robux item in the early weapon shop, mosquito spray, is entirely skippable.

**Should you feed every mosquito to 100%?**
Yes, at least early on. A mosquito pays only a fraction of its maximum until it's fully fed — one example started at 10 per second with a ceiling of 100. Feeding is the multiplier that turns a cheap hatch into real income.
