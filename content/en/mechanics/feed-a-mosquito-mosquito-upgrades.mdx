Standing in the shop with 35,000 cash and no clear answer is the fastest way to stall your income in Feed A Mosquito. Feed A Mosquito mosquito upgrades control three things at once — which field targets you can actually beat, how fast your blood regenerates, and how quickly a freshly hatched earner climbs to its full pay rate — and a wrong purchase can cost you hours of momentum. The good news is that Feed A Mosquito mosquito upgrades stop being guesswork once you follow one simple rule: buy whichever system is currently blocking your highest-paying mosquito.

That rule comes from two documented full playthroughs, and it holds up across every stage of the game. This guide breaks down the verified prices, the situations behind each decision, and the spots where player experience still disagrees.

## The One Rule Behind Every Feed A Mosquito Mosquito Upgrade Decision

The three main cash sinks are not competitors. They gate each other, which is why no single "best first buy" exists for every account.

| System | What it controls | Why it blocks your income |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapons | Which field mosquitoes you can defeat at all | Each tier opens stronger targets that pay more |
| Health station (starts as a chair) | Blood regeneration rate | The same blood pool is your survival meter *and* the fuel for feeding |
| Feeding | A mosquito's pay multiplier | A new mosquito starts near a tenth of its max rate, and feeding is free |

Feeding is the piece most players misunderstand. A mosquito worth 100 per second does not pay 100 when you hatch it — it starts around 10. In the documented runs, one mosquito's pay climbed from 10 to 26 to 50 per second while being fed, and a venomous mosquito went from 6 to 24. Feeding costs no cash, but it drains your own health, the drain scales with the quality of the mosquito, and you can only feed one at a time.

That last detail is the whole reason the priority keeps flipping. Your regen rate literally caps your income, because a better earner drains you faster than you can refill. So the loop becomes: a weapon step-up opens a new fight, health investment makes that fight survivable and lets you feed the new earner, and then the next weapon tier repeats the cycle.

| Stage | Cash level | What is blocking you | Documented buy |

### Mid-early game: broom before the life chair

At roughly 15,000–35,000 cash with the shoe equipped, one run hit a 2,000-HP field mosquito it could not outlast. The player had planned the health upgrade next but bought the broom first because damage was the hard blocker. The moment the broom landed, the priority flipped instantly — the mosquito could now be killed, but the player did not have the health to survive the fight. The next cash went straight into the health station at the 15,000 milestone.

The lesson: buy damage when damage is the wall, then switch to regen the second it stops being the wall.

### When feeding stalls: couch and bed before any weapon

A mythical mosquito already paying 100k per second at base shut one run down completely — the drain was faster than the regen, and the player openly admitted they could not feed it yet. The fix was not a weapon. It was the couch at 80 health per second, then the bed, and only then more feeding. After those step-ups, the run's demon mosquito was fed up to 220k per second. The same logic produced a toilet-IV upgrade to about 184 blood purely to prepare for feeding a large larva.

If your best earner drains faster than you regenerate, no weapon purchase helps. Skip the shop's weapon tab entirely until the station catches up.

### The housewife gate: the 500,000 pan plus a bench

The housewife mosquito demanded roughly 500 health in one run. With about 500,000 banked and the base already earning heavily, the player compared the 50,000 next weapon against the 500,000 frying pan, judged the gap affordable, and bought the pan — picking up a bench at 40 health per second along the way. The pan then beat the housewife cleanly.

At a boss gate, the documented answer is the boss-capable weapon *plus* the best regen tier you can afford alongside it, not one instead of the other. These decisions matter more than most Feed A Mosquito mosquito upgrades because a failed boss attempt wastes both time and blood.

### At the 300k/s plateau: electric swatter, then the Robux spray

With feeding maxed and total income around 300,000 per second — most of it from the demon mosquito — the run's own conclusion was blunt: something stronger was needed. The next cash tier was listed at 1,500,000, but the player bought the electric swatter instead, and regular mosquitoes became one-shots. The bosses that melee could not crack, the Yeti above all, went to the mosquito spray: 1,000 Robux for 10,000,000 damage that erased everything it touched.

## Where Player Experience Still Disagrees

Because every figure here comes from two single-session runs, some of it conflicts. Know these gaps before you plan a shopping spree.

| Unsettled point | What the runs show | What to do |

| Health station prices past 15,000 | Never displayed, and tier order differs between runs | Buy the next regen step you can afford, not a named piece of furniture |
| Cash weapon damage | Never stated on screen — only the spray's 10,000,000 appears | Judge by whether a target dies, not by a number |
| Plot expansion | Cost and slot count never shown | Treat it as an "as needed" buy when your base fills |
| Robux boosts | Only the 3x boost at 3 Robux is confirmed | Check whether active codes cover a boost for free first |

One more caution: Robux can also skip egg-hatch timers, but that is a time-saver, not a stat upgrade. The free 15,000-cash health route serves the same gating need without spending anything.

## Practical Habits That Keep Your Upgrades Cheap

- **Name your blocker before you shop.** Ask whether you are failing to kill, failing to survive, or failing to feed. The answer picks the tab for you.
- **Never buy a weapon during a feeding stall.** It is the single most common wasted purchase in community reports.
- **Bank for the gate, not the gap.** If a boss needs roughly 500 health, save until you can afford the weapon and a regen tier together.
- **Upgrade regen in steps, not jumps.** Bench to couch to bed is a steady climb that keeps feeding viable at every point.
- **Recheck prices after updates.** Balance changes can move any of these numbers, so verify in your own shop.

## FAQ

**What is the best first Feed A Mosquito mosquito upgrade?**
There is no universal answer. Buy the shoe early for baseline damage, then spend on whichever system is blocking your highest-paying mosquito — usually the broom around 35,000 cash if damage is the wall, or a regen step if feeding is the wall.

**Should I buy weapons or health first in the mid game?**
Whichever blocks you right now. In documented runs the broom came before the health upgrade, and the health upgrade came immediately after. They alternate rather than forming a fixed ladder.

**Are the health station furniture names important?**
No. Chair, toilet, hot tub, bathtub and bed are cosmetic labels across different runs. What matters is the regen rate you gain, with the 15,000-cash first step being the only price confirmed in both runs.

**Is the Robux mosquito spray worth it?**
Only for bosses that melee cannot crack, such as the Yeti. At 1,000 Robux for 10,000,000 damage it is a genuine stat upgrade, but it is not needed to clear regular content — the electric swatter handles that for cash.
